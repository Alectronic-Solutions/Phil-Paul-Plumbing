Warning Signs

Warning Signs Your Business Has a Plumbing Problem

Drains are slow or backing up across multiple fixtures.

When more than one drain is slow at the same time — restroom sinks, floor drains, or kitchen lines — the problem is almost never isolated to a single fixture. It usually means a blockage or buildup in the main sewer line serving your property. In commercial settings, this can escalate quickly. Grease accumulation in kitchen lines and heavy daily usage accelerate blockages that would take years to develop in a residential system. If multiple drains are running slowly, it’s worth having the sewer line inspected before a full backup forces a shutdown.

Water pressure has dropped noticeably in your building.

A sudden or gradual drop in water pressure across your facility can indicate one of several problems: a failing pressure regulator, a partial blockage in the supply line, a hidden leak reducing volume, or deteriorated pipe walls restricting flow. In older commercial buildings throughout Lodi and Stockton, galvanized supply lines corrode from the inside over time, progressively reducing pressure without any visible external sign. Low pressure in a commercial setting affects restrooms, kitchen operations, and equipment — it’s worth diagnosing early.

Your water bills have increased without a change in usage.

An unexplained increase in your commercial water bill almost always means water is being lost somewhere in the system. A running toilet in a commercial restroom can waste hundreds of gallons per day. A pinhole leak in a supply line within a wall or ceiling can go undetected for months, steadily increasing costs and causing concealed water damage. If your water bill has crept up and your usage patterns haven’t changed, a plumber can pressure-test the system and locate the source.

You’re seeing water stains, damp spots, or mold on walls or ceilings.

Discoloration on walls or ceilings, soft drywall, or visible mold growth near plumbing fixtures are signs of an active or historical leak somewhere inside the building envelope. In commercial buildings, these leaks are frequently found above drop ceilings, inside utility chases, or behind walls adjacent to restrooms and kitchen lines. Left unaddressed, they cause structural damage, create liability exposure, and can trigger health code concerns. This is the warning sign that warrants the most urgent response.
